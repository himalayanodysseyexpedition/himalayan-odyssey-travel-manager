Winter Spiti Tours

A winter journey through Spiti needs more flexibility than a regular summer road trip. Plan around the Shimla–Kinnaur approach, cold-weather preparation and verified local road updates.

Winter route planning via Shimla and Kinnaur

  • For a winter journey towards Kaza, plan around the Shimla–Kinnaur–Sumdo–Tabo road. It is the usual winter approach, but can still be disrupted by snow, ice, landslides or road work.
  • Do not rely on the Manali–Kunzum–Kaza route in winter. Kunzum Pass generally closes because of snow; check current official route information before departure.
  • A road open to Kaza does not guarantee every village road or local excursion is accessible.

Build a flexible winter itinerary

  • Allow overnight halts on the approach rather than attempting a long drive in one day.
  • Keep buffer time for weather and road delays. A fixed schedule with no spare day is not suitable for high-altitude winter travel.
  • Choose local sightseeing only after checking road conditions and the status of accommodation and local services.

Prepare for cold and altitude

  • Pack thermal base layers, insulating mid-layers, a windproof outer layer, warm gloves, a cap and suitable winter footwear.
  • Carry personal medicines and follow medical advice for high-altitude travel. Plan gradual ascent and rest; do not ignore symptoms of altitude illness.
  • Confirm heating arrangements and property facilities for your dates before paying or travelling.

Snowfall and access are not guaranteed

  • Snowfall timing and quantity vary from year to year and between villages.
  • Road access can change quickly. Follow district administration and police updates, and be prepared to delay or change the route.
  • Confirm what is included and what remains weather-dependent before the trip.

How a winter Spiti itinerary is usually paced

A winter plan should prioritise safe road travel and time to adjust to altitude. A sample sequence may include an approach halt around Narkanda or Kinnaur, onward travel towards Tabo and Kaza, rest and local exploration around Kaza, then a staged return. The exact overnight stops must be decided using current road conditions and available accommodation.

  • Keep the first days for the approach and avoid scheduling several long mountain drives back-to-back.
  • Keep a flexible day around Kaza so weather or local access changes do not force unsafe driving.
  • Do not promise visits to high villages, viewpoints or remote roads until local authorities and operators confirm access.
  • Set a realistic return buffer. A fixed flight or train connection immediately after the planned road journey can be risky.

Winter Spiti booking checklist

  • Confirm the vehicle type and whether the local sightseeing vehicle is the same as the transfer vehicle.
  • Ask each stay about room heating, hot water, meals, backup power and what is actually available on your dates.
  • Carry layered winter clothing, suitable footwear, personal medicines and essential items in a day bag.
  • Agree how route changes, extra nights, delays and cancellations caused by weather or official road restrictions will be handled.

Frequently asked questions

Is Spiti open in winter?

The Shimla–Kinnaur approach is the usual winter road connection towards Kaza, but it can be interrupted by snow, ice, landslides and road work. Check official updates before travel.

Can I travel from Manali to Kaza in winter?

Do not plan on crossing Kunzum Pass in winter. The Manali–Kunzum route generally closes due to snow; verify official route status.

Will I definitely see snowfall?

No. Snowfall depends on weather and timing and cannot be guaranteed for a particular date or village.
