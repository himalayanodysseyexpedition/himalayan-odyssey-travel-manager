Winter Spiti Tour 2027: Routes, Snow, Stays and Trip Planning

A winter journey to Spiti is not simply a summer itinerary with snow added. It is a slower, more weather-dependent trip through a high-altitude cold desert, where road access, accommodation, daylight and local conditions shape every day. This guide explains how to plan a responsible Winter Spiti tour in 2027.

What makes Winter Spiti different?

From late autumn through winter, Spiti's villages and mountain slopes can be covered in snow, temperatures can fall well below freezing and services may operate on reduced schedules. The landscape is striking, but the experience is more demanding than a regular summer road trip. Travellers should be comfortable with cold conditions, flexible plans and the possibility of waiting out a weather-related road closure.

Himachal Tourism describes Spiti as a high-altitude region where temperatures can drop sharply, especially in December, January and February. Warm clothing is essential even outside the deepest winter months. Read the official Himachal Tourism Spiti guide before finalising your dates.

Which route should you plan for winter?

For a winter trip to Kaza, plan around the Shimla–Kinnaur–Sumdo–Tabo route. The Lahaul and Spiti district administration describes the Shimla-to-Kaza road as an almost all-weather connection, while the Keylong-to-Kaza route over Kunzum Pass generally remains closed during the winter season because of snow. “Almost all-weather” does not mean guaranteed access: snowfall, landslides, ice or road work can still interrupt travel.

Do not plan a winter circuit that depends on crossing Kunzum Pass or visiting Chandratal. Check the district administration's current route guidance and local road updates immediately before departure.

How many days should you keep?

Keep more time than the minimum map estimate suggests. A practical winter plan usually includes a gradual approach through Shimla and Kinnaur, an overnight stop around Narkanda, Rampur, Kalpa or Reckong Peo depending on road progress, then the onward journey through Nako and Tabo towards Kaza. The return should use the same Kinnaur side unless authorities announce a safe alternative.

For a first winter visit, avoid trying to cover every village in a fixed number of days. Add buffer days for weather and keep sightseeing flexible. A short itinerary that leaves no room for a delay can become stressful or unsafe.

What can you see in winter?

Depending on road access and local conditions, travellers may explore Kaza and nearby settlements, visit monasteries when they are open to visitors, experience the winter landscape around Kibber or Langza, and spend time learning about local life. None of these excursions should be treated as guaranteed. A road that is open to Kaza does not automatically mean every village road is safe or passable.

Clothing and essential preparation

  • Use thermal base layers, insulated mid-layers and a windproof outer jacket.
  • Carry warm socks, gloves, a cap that covers your ears and waterproof winter footwear with good grip.
  • Keep essential medicines, personal prescriptions, a basic first-aid kit and a personal water bottle accessible.
  • Carry offline maps, a power bank and some cash; mobile connectivity and digital payments can be inconsistent.
  • Pack light but do not compromise on insulation. Keep one spare warm layer in your day bag.

Choose transport and stays carefully

Winter mountain driving requires a vehicle suited to the actual road and weather conditions, an experienced local mountain driver and a realistic daily schedule. Ask who will make the final go/no-go decision if the road becomes unsafe. Confirm accommodation directly for each night, including heating arrangements, hot water availability, meals and cancellation terms. Do not assume every summer hotel or café remains open in winter.

Who should choose a winter trip?

Winter Spiti is best for travellers who value quiet landscapes and cultural experiences, can handle cold weather and accept changes to the plan. It may not suit travellers who need a strict timetable, guaranteed access to every attraction or extensive comfort facilities at every stop.

Frequently asked questions

Is Spiti accessible in winter?

The Shimla–Kinnaur approach is the usual winter road approach to Kaza, but it can still be disrupted by snow, landslides and ice. Check current district road updates before travelling.

Can I travel from Manali to Kaza in winter?

Do not plan on the Manali–Kunzum–Kaza route in winter. Kunzum Pass generally closes due to snow; verify current official route status.

How many days are enough for Winter Spiti?

Allow a flexible multi-day itinerary with overnight halts and buffer days. The right duration depends on road conditions, starting point and which local excursions are open.
