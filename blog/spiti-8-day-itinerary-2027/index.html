Before using this 8-day route

This is a planning framework, not a promise that every road or village will be accessible. The Shimla-to-Kaza road via Kinnaur is described by the district administration as an almost all-weather connection subject to prevailing conditions. The Manali–Kaza route over Kunzum Pass is seasonal and generally closes in winter. Check the official district route guidance before travel.

For winter travel, this itinerary should be made more flexible and may require extra buffer days. For summer, a Manali exit can be considered only when Kunzum Pass is officially open and the road is suitable.

Day 1: Delhi to Shimla or Narkanda

Start early from Delhi and travel towards Shimla or Narkanda. This is a long road day, so avoid adding unnecessary sightseeing. Overnight at the selected stop based on your arrival time and road conditions.

Day 2: Narkanda to Kinnaur

Continue through the Sutlej valley towards Rampur and Kinnaur. Choose an overnight stay around Reckong Peo or Kalpa depending on the plan, weather and available accommodation. Keep the day flexible for road works and breaks.

Day 3: Kinnaur to Tabo

Travel via Nako and Sumdo towards Tabo. This is a mountain transfer day; start in daylight and allow time for food and rest stops. If road conditions are poor, the overnight plan should be changed rather than pushing to reach a fixed destination late.

Day 4: Tabo to Kaza

Continue to Kaza, settle into your accommodation and keep the remainder of the day light. This is not the day to schedule a demanding excursion. Use the time to rest, hydrate and adjust to the altitude.

Day 5: Kaza and nearby sights

Explore Kaza and nearby attractions that are accessible on the day. Depending on conditions and local opening hours, this may include a monastery visit or a short village excursion. Do not assume that all high villages are connected by clear roads in every season.

Day 6: Local exploration or buffer day

Use this day for a locally approved excursion, or keep it as a buffer if the weather has affected earlier travel. A flexible day is valuable in Spiti because road conditions can change quickly.

Day 7: Kaza to Kinnaur

Begin the return via Tabo and the Kinnaur route. Choose an overnight stop based on daylight and current road status. Avoid driving late simply to preserve the original schedule.

Day 8: Kinnaur to Shimla and onward

Continue towards Shimla and then onward to Delhi if conditions and timing permit. For many groups, a ninth day or an overnight stop closer to Shimla makes the return more comfortable. Do not book a non-refundable onward connection with an unrealistically tight margin.

Can this itinerary include the Manali circuit?

Only in the season when the route over Kunzum Pass has officially opened and remains suitable for your vehicle. The pass opening is weather-dependent and cannot be guaranteed for a future date. A circuit should be planned as a conditional option, not as a fixed promise.

Practical tips for the journey

  • Prefer daylight driving and experienced mountain drivers.
  • Keep buffer time and avoid overloading each day with sightseeing.
  • Confirm accommodation, meals and winter operating arrangements.
  • Carry warm layers, medicines, offline maps and cash.
  • Respect local advice and official restrictions, especially near border areas.
  • Build the route around the slowest traveller in your group.

Frequently asked questions

Is 8 days enough for Spiti from Delhi?

It can be tight. It depends on road conditions and how much time you want in Kaza. A longer trip with buffer days is more resilient.

Can I visit Chandratal on this 8-day itinerary?

Only when the seasonal road and lake access are open and safe. Chandratal is not a winter stop.

Should I enter Spiti via Shimla or Manali?

The Shimla–Kinnaur route is the usual winter approach and offers a more gradual entry. The Manali route depends on seasonal opening of Kunzum Pass.
