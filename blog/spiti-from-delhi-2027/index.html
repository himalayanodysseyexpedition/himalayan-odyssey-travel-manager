How to Plan a Spiti Valley Trip from Delhi in 2027

A Spiti Valley trip from Delhi needs more planning than a regular hill-station weekend. The distance is long, road conditions change with the season and the two main approaches are not interchangeable. Use this guide to choose a route, build realistic travel days and prepare before you leave.

By Himalayan Odyssey Expedition · Published 20 November 2026 · Updated 25 November 2026 · Planning guide for 2027

A Spiti Valley road trip from Delhi generally needs around 8–10 days for a compact itinerary with gradual ascent and return travel. The driving distance depends on whether you approach Kaza via Shimla and Kinnaur or the seasonal Manali–Kunzum route, and on your overnight stops. In winter, plan around the Shimla–Kinnaur approach. Travellers joining a shared departure can also compare Spiti tours from Chandigarh before booking onward transport.

Start by choosing the right season

Your dates determine which road approach is practical. The Shimla–Kinnaur route is the usual approach when the Manali–Kunzum road is closed by snow. In the warmer season, the Manali side may open after snow clearance, allowing a circuit itinerary when authorities permit. The opening and closing dates vary each year.

Check the district administration's route information and the Himachal Tourism Spiti guide before selecting your route.

Option 1: Delhi to Spiti via Shimla and Kinnaur

This route travels from Delhi towards Shimla and then follows the Sutlej valley through Narkanda, Rampur and Kinnaur before continuing via Nako and Tabo to Kaza. It is longer than the direct summer approach from Manali, but it is the normal route to consider in winter and during periods when Kunzum Pass is closed.

Plan an overnight halt rather than treating the drive as one continuous transfer. A stop around Narkanda or further into Kinnaur can make the journey more manageable, depending on the start time and road conditions. For a first visit, a gradual approach is preferable to rushing to high altitude.

Option 2: Delhi to Spiti via Manali

When the seasonal road is officially open, travellers may reach Kaza from Manali via Atal Tunnel, Gramphu, Batal and Kunzum Pass. This route is shorter on the map but includes a high mountain pass and rough, weather-sensitive sections. It should only be attempted when authorities confirm access and the vehicle and driver are suitable.

Do not confuse the opening of Atal Tunnel with the opening of the entire route to Kaza. Kunzum Pass is the key seasonal constraint.

How many days should you keep?

For a first trip from Delhi, allow enough time for the long road transfers, overnight halts, time in Kaza and a return journey. A compact itinerary can be built around 8–10 days, but the right duration depends on season, route, road status and whether you want to include Kinnaur or a Manali exit. Winter travel needs additional flexibility and buffer days.

What should a first-time itinerary include?

  • Approach day: Delhi to Shimla/Narkanda or another suitable overnight stop.
  • Kinnaur days: Continue through the Sutlej valley and choose a sensible overnight halt based on road progress.
  • Entry to Spiti: Travel via Nako and Tabo towards Kaza, allowing breaks and daylight driving.
  • Local exploration: Visit accessible monasteries and villages only after checking local road conditions.
  • Return: Retrace the Kinnaur route in winter; consider the Manali exit only when Kunzum Pass is officially open and conditions are suitable.

Transport: bus, private cab or self-drive?

Public transport can be economical but requires flexibility around schedules and connections. A private cab gives more control over stops and overnight halts, but the vehicle, driver experience and daily driving plan matter. Self-driving is best left to people experienced with remote mountain roads, changing surfaces and high-altitude conditions. Do not choose a vehicle solely on its advertised category; confirm its actual suitability for the planned route.

Acclimatisation and health preparation

Spiti is a high-altitude destination. Avoid a rushed itinerary that climbs rapidly without rest. Stay hydrated, avoid heavy exertion on arrival and learn the symptoms of altitude illness. Anyone with a medical concern should seek advice from a qualified clinician before travelling. If symptoms worsen, descend and seek medical assistance rather than pushing on to the next stop.

What to budget for

Build your budget around transport, accommodation, meals, fuel, local sightseeing, permits if applicable, travel insurance and a contingency reserve. Winter stays and vehicle requirements can differ from summer. Ask for an itemised quote with inclusions, exclusions, cancellation terms and any extra charges for route changes. Avoid relying on an old online package price for a future departure.

Frequently asked questions

How far is Spiti from Delhi?

The distance depends on the route and destination point. The district administration publishes route distances from Shimla and other access points; allow multiple road days from Delhi.

Can I do Spiti in 5 days from Delhi?

Five days is generally too rushed for a safe, meaningful road trip from Delhi. Allow time for transfers, acclimatisation, sightseeing and possible delays.

Which route is better for a first Spiti trip?

The Shimla–Kinnaur approach offers a more gradual entry and is the usual winter route. The Manali approach is seasonal and depends on Kunzum Pass opening.
